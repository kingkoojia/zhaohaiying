Can I Get a Divorce Without Returning to China?

Family Law Updated: November 15, 2026

Short answer: the answer depends on the procedure. Article 1076 of the Civil Code of the PRC generally requires both parties to apply in person to the marriage registration authority, so a certified power of attorney does not make administrative divorce fully remote. Article 65 of the Civil Procedure Law provides that in divorce litigation the party is in principle still required to appear even where a litigation agent acts; if the party cannot express intention or another special circumstance applies, the court decides whether video participation or another arrangement is possible. Overseas documents may require notarisation, an Apostille or consular legalisation, but acceptance still depends on the registration authority or court.

Divorce routes

The main routes are administrative divorce, litigation in China, and recognition or other China-side handling of an overseas divorce judgment. They do not share one rule on personal appearance.

Divorce litigation

You may authorise a Chinese lawyer to act in the steps that can be delegated. Article 65 of the Civil Procedure Law provides that the party is in principle still required to appear; the court decides whether a statutory exception or video participation applies. Article 1079 of the Civil Code requires the court to attempt mediation and provides for divorce where the marriage relationship has irretrievably broken down and mediation fails.

Key documents

Marriage certificate, identification, property and family evidence, plus any authentication, translation and identity-consistency documents required by the receiving body.

FAQ

Q: Can I always divorce in China without returning?

A: No. Administrative divorce generally requires both parties to apply in person. In divorce litigation, the party is in principle still required to appear; whether a statutory exception or video participation applies is decided by the court.

Q: How can a divorce document signed abroad be used in China?

A: The document usually needs local notarisation and an Apostille or consular legalisation, plus a Chinese translation where required. Authentication proves formal matters such as signature and capacity; it does not mean that the registration authority or court must accept the document's substance.
