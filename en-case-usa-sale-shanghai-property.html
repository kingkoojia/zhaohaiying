Overseas Chinese Selling Shanghai Property: Cross-Border Identity Verification and Fund Repatriation

Foreign Property Case Updated: August 12, 2026

For foreign property owners, selling real estate in China is rarely the hard part. The real challenges are three: proving that your old passport and new passport belong to the same person, completing signing and title transfer while you are abroad, and lawfully repatriating the sale proceeds. This article follows the anonymized case of H, a Chinese-American woman who sold her Shanghai apartment, and explains the full cross-border process and the key documents involved.

1. Case Background

H, a U.S. national of Chinese origin, purchased an apartment in Shanghai with her son years ago. When they decided to sell, both were living in the United States and could not return to China. The passport numbers recorded on the original title had also been replaced long ago.

2. Key Difficulties

  1. Identity mismatch: the passport number on the title was the old passport; both owners had already renewed their passports, so the transaction center could not verify identity;
  2. Minor-to-adult transition: the son was a minor at purchase, requiring an explanation of the guardian identity and the later adult identity;
  3. Absence from China: listing, signing, tax review, registration, invoicing, banking, and foreign exchange steps all required authorized representation;
  4. Large fund repatriation: the sale price exceeded RMB 9 million, requiring tax payment and compliant purchase and remittance of foreign exchange;
  5. Long document chain: foreign documents had to go through notarization, Apostille, domestic translation, and notarization that the translation conformed to the original; any missing link could cause rejection.

3. Case Process

Step 1: Prepare identity and authorization documents abroad

  1. H signed an identity consistency certificate before a U.S. notary, listing the old and new passport numbers and validity periods;
  2. The son signed the identity consistency certificate and the power of attorney to the lawyer through remote online notarization;
  3. The Secretary of State issued an Apostille for the documents.

Step 2: Authentication and translation in China

  1. A qualified translation agency translated the power of attorney and identity certificate;
  2. The notary office notarized that the translation conformed to the original;
  3. The notary office issued a separate identity certificate notarization stating that it was solely for the sale of the property;
  4. A specific power of attorney was issued for handling the translation-conformity notarization.

Step 3: Listing, signing, tax review, and registration

  1. The property was listed through an agency;
  2. The parties signed the Shanghai Real Estate Sale and Purchase Contract;
  3. Tax filing for the second-hand property transaction was completed, and the tax payment certificate and invoice were obtained;
  4. Real property registration was completed, and the buyer obtained the new title certificate.

Step 4: Purchase of foreign exchange and repatriation

  1. All documents were assembled: contract, invoice, tax certificate, property information registration form, identity and authorization documents;
  2. Tax filing and purchase/payment of foreign exchange were completed under the foreign exchange regulations;
  3. The sale proceeds were remitted to the overseas account.

4. Legal Analysis

4.1 Same-person certificate for old and new passports

Situation: the passport number in the property records does not match the new passport.

Risk: identity verification fails and title transfer cannot proceed; non-compliant documents are repeatedly rejected by the notary office and transaction center.

Recommendation: complete notarization in the United States, Apostille by the Secretary of State, domestic translation, and translation-conformity notarization; if necessary, request a notarized identity certificate.

4.2 Scope of authorization in the overseas power of attorney

Situation: the owner cannot return to China and needs a lawyer to handle all steps.

Risk: a power of attorney that is not notarized and Apostilled is not accepted in China; incomplete authorization causes long delays and extra costs.

Recommendation: the authorization should cover signing, registration, tax review, invoicing, bank account opening/transfer/closure, foreign exchange approval and purchase/payment, receiving documents, and sub-authorization; state the validity period and sub-authorization rights clearly.

4.3 Repatriation of sale proceeds

Situation: large sale proceeds need to be remitted abroad.

Risk: incomplete documents can leave funds trapped in China; poor coordination between tax and foreign exchange steps affects receipt of payment.

Recommendation: complete tax filing and payment first and obtain the invoice; prepare tax filing materials; confirm the bank's requirements for foreign exchange purchase and payment in advance.

5. Recommendations for Clients

  1. Confirm requirements before signing: check the format requirements of the notary office and transaction center before signing the identity certificate;
  2. Authorize broadly, not narrowly: in particular, include bank, foreign exchange, and sub-authorization powers;
  3. Prepare the three-document set: overseas notarization + Apostille + domestic translation notarization, and reuse the set throughout;
  4. All co-owners must authorize: every owner must complete identity certification and authorization; handle minor issues at the same time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: What is an Apostille certificate?

A: Under the Convention Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for Foreign Public Documents, effective in China since November 7, 2023, an Apostille issued by a U.S. Secretary of State replaces consular legalization and is accepted directly in China.

Q2: Can I authorize a lawyer to sell my property in China while I am abroad?

A: Yes. After notarizing and Apostilling a power of attorney, you can remotely authorize a Chinese lawyer to handle listing, signing, tax review, registration, payment, and foreign exchange procedures without returning to China.

Q3: Must the sale proceeds be remitted overseas?

A: No. Remittance is optional. If you choose to repatriate, tax filing and foreign exchange procedures must be completed under the applicable regulations, and it is advisable to authorize these steps in the power of attorney in advance.

Q4: What if the property is co-owned by multiple people?

A: All owners must complete identity consistency certification and authorization. If an owner purchased the property as a minor, the guardian identity and the later adult identity must also be reconciled.
