Inherited China property—what order for sale and remittance docs?

Title · Tax clearance · FX remittance

Fund Transfer Published: September 14, 2026 · Updated: November 16, 2026

Short answer: first determine the heir's legal status and the nature of the inherited funds, then prepare title, sale, tax and FX steps in the right order. Under Article 2 of the Interim Measures and the 2024 Capital Account FX Business Guide, an inheritance transfer is available to foreign citizens and Hong Kong or Macao residents. A Chinese citizen who holds permanent residence abroad does not, by that status alone, qualify as an inheritance-transfer applicant. Emigration transfer is principally for the applicant's own mainland property held before acquiring the immigration status, and inherited property should not be placed directly in that route. Once the route is identified, the relevant approval, bank review and payment steps can be checked. No outcome or arrival date is promised.

Overseas heirs often treat inheritance and remittance after a sale as one step. The usual practical order is to complete inheritance and registration, sell and settle tax, then handle FX under the route that actually fits the applicant. An applicant who falls within the inheritance-transfer category applies to the bureau at the deceased's last registered residence. A person outside that category needs a separate case-specific analysis of household registration, migration facts, when the property was acquired and the local branch's current approach.

Identify the category first. Under Article 2, an inheritance transfer is the act of a foreign citizen or a resident of Hong Kong or Macao converting an inherited mainland estate and remitting the proceeds. An emigration transfer covers people who moved from the mainland and are remitting their own lawful property held before taking up that residence. Article 3 requires the property to be lawful and free of competing claims. Permanent residence abroad, without foreign nationality or a Hong Kong/Macao status, is not enough to make an heir an inheritance-transfer applicant; inherited property is also not automatically an emigration-transfer asset.

The annual USD 50,000 personal facilitation quota is not a statutory substitute for an outward transfer of inherited property. In bank practice, a small inherited sum may be reviewed as a genuine current-account transaction, such as family maintenance, but this does not mean that a large inheritance or inherited property-sale proceeds may be split over years to use the facilitation quota. Dividing the payment among multiple people, spreading it across years or splitting individual amounts to avoid capital-account administration is not permitted. The bank and SAFE assess the genuine transaction background and supporting documents case by case.

Who this helps

  • Heirs who have inherited, or are still inheriting, China real estate and plan to sell, then remit;
  • Post-inheritance equity liquidation and similar paths—those cross more tax and registration steps; this page only flags the bridge and does not expand them;
  • Heirs abroad who prefer to proceed by remote power of attorney rather than returning in person.

Why three steps

  1. Title in the heir’s name: a notarial inheritance path, litigation, or a direct application by the heirs to the real-estate registration authority with the relevant materials, then property registration;
  2. Sale and tax clearance: contract, transfer, and tax certificates as assessed by the tax authority;
  3. Route-specific approval or arrangement, then the bank: an inheritance-transfer applicant applies under Article 5 to the bureau at the deceased's last registered residence and buys foreign exchange at a designated bank against the approval. All inherited property from the same deceased should be submitted as one complete application after conversion and may be paid in one transfer or in instalments within the approved scope. Under Article 6 of SAFE Notice Huifa [2014] No. 2, property inherited from different deceased persons may be included in a combined application filed with the bureau at the last registered residence of one of the deceased. The approval's validity and usable period follow the document and the local branch and bank requirements.

Reversing the order—listing or signing before inheritance is done, or applying to remit before tax clearance—usually leaves the seller, title, and tax certificates out of line. Banks and registries commonly stall the file there.

Sale and tax

Typical items include the sale contract, title or transfer proof, and tax clearance certificates. Whether tax is payable on a sale after inheritance is assessed by the tax authority under the rules in force at the time of sale.

FX side

Bank lists usually cover identity, transaction and tax papers, and a source-of-funds note; branches do not use one nationwide pack. Five common stalls: China property sale remittance pitfalls. Liquidation and remittance overview: asset liquidation and fund repatriation.

Common stalls (bridge)

  • Listing or signing before inheritance is done: the heir is not yet the registered owner, so an ordinary resale path usually cannot complete (Civil Code, Articles 230 and 232: ownership acquired by inheritance takes effect when the succession opens; disposing of the immovable property requires registration first);
  • Heirs disagree and title stays stuck: if notarization cannot move, litigation or another path may be needed; sale and FX then wait;
  • Tax certificate vs seller ID mismatch: heir name or passport spelling that does not match the tax ticket or contract often triggers a bank query or supplement;
  • Thin source-of-funds for a large amount or multiple assets: banks may ask for a fuller inheritance, sale, and tax trail. This page does not promise a one-shot remittance;
  • Underestimating Apostille and POA time: signing abroad, notarization, Apostille or other authentication, and mailing originals takes a length of time that varies widely from case to case.

Timing

Inheritance registration, sale and tax clearance, and bank FX are stacked segments, not one clock. Foreign authentication, supplements, multiple heirs, and large or multiple assets each add time. Those ranges are not promises of processing time or arrival.

Frequently asked questions

Q: Can I take the sale proceeds straight to a bank and remit them?

A: Usually not. An inheritance transfer has to be approved by the foreign exchange bureau first, and only then can a designated bank buy the foreign exchange and remit it (Article 5 of the Interim Measures). A bank will generally not take a sale contract and a tax certificate on their own.

Q: Which foreign exchange bureau takes the application?

A: For an inheritance transfer, the bureau at the place where the deceased was last registered as a resident. The central sub-branch of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange where the applicant is located may receive the application documents on its behalf (Article 5 of the Interim Measures).

Q: Can the money be sent in instalments?

A: All inherited property from the same deceased should be submitted as one complete application after conversion. After approval, payment may be made in one transfer or in instalments within the approved scope. Under Article 6 of SAFE Notice Huifa [2014] No. 2, property inherited from different deceased persons no longer has to be applied for and remitted separately; a combined application may be filed with the bureau at the last registered residence of one of the deceased. The approval's validity and usable period follow the document and the local branch and bank requirements.

Q: What documents does the application require?

A: Under Article 9 of the Interim Measures, an inheritance transfer application normally includes a written application, the application form for foreign exchange business involving transfer of personal property, identity documents, proof of the property right, a tax certificate or tax payment receipt issued by the tax authority, and, where an agent acts, the agency agreement and the agent's identity document. The bureau's current checklist governs.

Q: I hold a Chinese passport and a U.S. green card. After inheriting and selling a Shanghai property, can I remit the sale proceeds directly?

A: Usually, a U.S. green card cannot be treated as qualification for an inheritance transfer. Inheritance rights are not lost merely because the heir holds a green card, but Article 2 of the Interim Measures and the 2024 Capital Account FX Business Guide describe an inheritance-transfer applicant as a foreign citizen or a Hong Kong or Macao resident. A Chinese citizen holding only a U.S. permanent resident card does not, by that status alone, qualify as an inheritance-transfer applicant, and inherited property should not be placed directly in the emigration-transfer route. The annual USD 50,000 facilitation quota is not a substitute for large inherited sale proceeds; splitting a payment among people, across years or into smaller amounts to avoid capital-account administration is not permitted. The route depends on household registration, migration facts, documents showing when and how the property was acquired, sale and tax records, the nature of the funds and review by the local SAFE branch and bank.
