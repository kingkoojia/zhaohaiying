China Inheritance Checklist for U.S.-Based Families

Inheritance Checklist Updated: November 15, 2026

Short answer: before approaching a Chinese bank or signing a property sale, a U.S.-based family should organize the facts around six issues: the deceased and heirs, family relationships, any will, China-situs assets, the available inheritance procedure and the later fund-transfer route. Chinese law governs China-situs property, but the route still depends on the deceased's habitual residence, the location and type of assets, any dispute, and the heir's legal status.

1. Start with a one-page family fact sheet

A written fact sheet prevents the family from collecting documents in the wrong order.

  • The deceased's name, nationality, last habitual residence, place and date of death;
  • Whether the spouse, children and parents are alive, including step-family, adoptive relationships or other facts that may affect succession;
  • Any will, testamentary trust arrangement, mutual will, marital property agreement or estate-planning document, and who holds the original;
  • China-situs property, bank deposits, securities, funds, company equity or other assets;
  • Where each heir lives, whether anyone is missing, refuses to cooperate or disputes the will or shares;
  • Any inheritance notarisation, litigation, property registration, bank withdrawal or sale already completed.

2. Check identity and family-relationship documents

The most common problem is not a complete absence of documents, but inconsistent names, dates, identity numbers or family relationships across documents.

  • The deceased's death certificate; if issued abroad, authentication and Chinese translation follow the issuing state and receiving body's requirements;
  • Identity documents for the deceased and every heir;
  • Household register, birth certificate, marriage certificate, one-child records, personnel file or other kinship evidence;
  • Former Chinese ID, household-registration cancellation, former-name record or passport-change documents;
  • Additional evidence for adoption, step-relations, marital changes or other family facts.

Article 1127 of the Civil Code of the PRC sets out the statutory order of inheritance. Receiving bodies usually require documentary evidence that cross-checks, not only a family statement.

3. Find the will and check whether anyone disputes it

A will does not by itself authorise a property registry to transfer title where another heir challenges its authenticity, form or effect. A substantive ownership dispute generally requires litigation or another confirmation procedure.

  1. Locate the original will and all attachments;
  2. Check the form, date, witnesses and custody record;
  3. Check whether a later will, revocation or amendment exists;
  4. Confirm whether every heir accepts the will;
  5. If the will or shares are disputed, assess litigation before relying on registration.

4. Inventory the China-situs estate

Unknown accounts should first be organised by asset type and available documentary clues.

  • Real estate: title certificate, purchase contract, address, registered owners, co-owners and mortgages;
  • Bank deposits: bank, account clues, deposit slips, cards and historical statements;
  • Securities and funds: institution, account clues, holdings and statements;
  • Company equity: company name, unified social credit code, articles of association, capital contribution and registration records;
  • Other assets: vehicles, insurance, receivables and digital assets, depending on the available title evidence.

5. Choose the inheritance procedure

Inheritance registration is not limited to notarisation. Under Article 14 of the Implementing Rules for the Interim Regulations on Real Estate Registration, an applicant may submit notarial inheritance materials, an effective legal document, or the death certificate, will or heirs' agreement and kinship materials required by the applicable procedure.

  • No dispute and complete documents: assess inheritance notarisation or the local non-notarial registration procedure;
  • Refusal, missing heir or dispute over the will or shares: assess litigation or another confirmation procedure;
  • Assets in different Chinese cities: check each registry and bank separately; Shanghai practice is not a national template.

6. Decide whether documents need to be handled remotely

Whether a U.S.-based heir must travel depends on the institution, step and document type. No page can promise that every step is fully remote.

  • A power of attorney may require notarisation, an Apostille or consular legalisation and a Chinese translation, depending on the issuing state and receiving body;
  • A waiver of inheritance must be made in writing by the heir before the estate is distributed; silence is not a waiver;
  • Overseas remote video notarisation is available only at some institutions, for some countries and for some matters;
  • In divorce or inheritance litigation, personal appearance may still be required unless the court accepts a statutory exception.

7. Selling inherited property and transferring proceeds are separate questions

Inheritance rights do not automatically create inheritance-transfer eligibility for foreign-exchange purposes. Under Article 2 of the Interim Measures for the Administration of the Sale and Purchase of Foreign Exchange for the Transfer of Personal Property Abroad and the 2024 Capital Account FX Business Guide, an inheritance transfer is available to foreign citizens and Hong Kong or Macao residents.

  • A Chinese citizen who holds only a U.S. green card does not, by that status alone, qualify for an inheritance transfer;
  • Inherited property should not be placed directly in the emigration-transfer route;
  • The annual USD 50,000 personal facilitation quota is not a substitute for an inheritance transfer;
  • All inherited property from the same deceased should be submitted as one complete application after conversion; property inherited from different deceased persons may be included in a combined application under SAFE Notice Huifa [2014] No. 2;
  • The approval's validity and usable period follow the document and the local SAFE branch and bank.

8. Ten checks the family can complete now

  1. Create a list of every heir and family member;
  2. Save the death certificate, will, title documents and bank records in separate folders;
  3. List the Chinese and English names, birth dates and identity numbers on each document;
  4. Mark every name or date mismatch;
  5. Identify whether anyone disputes the will, shares or sale;
  6. Confirm the city and registered owner of each China asset;
  7. Do not send full passport numbers, identity numbers or bank account numbers in a first inquiry;
  8. Do not list a property for sale before inheritance and registration are understood;
  9. Do not apply to remit funds before confirming the heir's legal-status route;
  10. Record every notarisation, authentication, registration and banking step by date.

Frequently asked questions

Q: What should a U.S.-based family prepare first for a China inheritance?

A: Start with a one-page family fact sheet covering the deceased, all heirs, marriage and kinship facts, wills and China-situs assets. Do not send full passport numbers, identity numbers, bank account numbers or highly sensitive documents in a first inquiry.

Q: Must every heir travel to China to complete the inheritance?

A: Not necessarily. Where the documents, identity verification and receiving body's rules permit, some steps may be handled through foreign notarisation, an Apostille or overseas remote video notarisation. Acceptance and attendance requirements depend on the notary, court, property registry and bank.

Q: Can inherited funds be transferred using the annual USD 50,000 quota?

A: No. The annual personal facilitation quota is not a statutory substitute for transferring inherited property abroad. Eligibility first depends on the heir's legal status and the nature of the funds. A Chinese citizen holding only foreign permanent residence does not, by that status alone, qualify for an inheritance transfer.
